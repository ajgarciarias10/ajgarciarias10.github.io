🏎️ F1-BUGAMBRA

Live in production Installable PWA

A championship between friends, managed like a real Formula 1 team.

It started as a console league on Sunday nights. It ended up as a full management platform: contracts, team budgets, a transfer market with real money rules, race-by-race auditing and standings that rebuild themselves from scratch. Every weekend a group of real drivers opens the app, checks their seat, negotiates a transfer and watches the championship move.

The interesting part is how it was built: the business logic was designed by an engineer — rulesets, economy, market parameters, immutable race revisions — and the product itself was shipped with vibe coding, iterating against real feedback from the league instead of a spec document. Design decisions had to survive contact with twenty people who notice immediately when a single point is wrong.

From a spreadsheet to a product

Building this page — and the platform behind it — was a personal design decision, not a client brief. Nobody asked for it: I chose the architecture, the rules and the look because I wanted the league to run on something better than goodwill.

At the start of the championship the regulations lived in an Excel sheet we wrote together: points, budgets, penalties, contract lengths and transfer limits, one tab per topic, updated by hand after every race. It worked — until two people opened it at once and the standings stopped agreeing with each other.

This site systematises those same rules. Every column of that spreadsheet became a validated rule in code, and the whole thing moved to an improved web version: one source of truth, live for everyone, with the history kept instead of overwritten.

Extra · Points admin, economy and live auction

Beyond the league itself, the hard parts were the ones nobody sees: an administration panel that closes a race and recomputes every classification, an economy where each team has a budget, a salary bill and clauses that have to add up to the last euro, and an auction mode for signing free drivers.

For the auction I built a real-time application that simulated a live bidding war between several teams at once. Everyone joins from their own phone, every bid is written to Firestore and lands on all the other screens instantly, a shared countdown extends itself when someone bids at the last second, and the budget is validated before the bid is accepted — so two teams can never spend the same money. It turned a chaotic WhatsApp thread into an auction room that runs itself.

  • Installable on any phone as a PWA, with an install guide written for people who are not developers.
